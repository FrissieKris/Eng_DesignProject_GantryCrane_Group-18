EERI I Drive Electroncics by Jopie Roux.

Table \ref{tab1:interfaces-eeri1} and \ref{tab2:interfaces-eeri1} define the the external inteerfaces that 
cross the boundary of the drive electronics (F/U 1.1) and the internal interfaces bewtween
its level 2 functional units. The interface type letters correspond to figure \ref{fig:arch-eeri1}, where E denotes
electrical interfaces, S signal interfaces and M mechanical interfaces. 
The drive-electronics subsystem receives single-phase AC, distributes protected power to the 
required PSUs, receives control signals from REII, and supplies the three-phase induction 
motor used by MCTR II. It also returns conditioned current feedback to the controller and provides local 
hardware protection of the inverter.

The main power path flows from the protected mains to the bridge rectifier, to the DC bus for smoothing using a bulk
capacitor to supply the 3-phase inverter. In parallel, a 12VDC PSU powers the gate driver ICs which use the isolated 
PWM singals from REII to switch the IBTSs and create the 3-phase AC for the induction motor. The EEII I subsystem
also provides a current-sense signal to REII for feedback and local protection of the inverter. 

\subsection{EERI II: Magnetic Load Attachment Interfaces}
\label{subsec:eeri2-interfaces}
The EERI II subsystem interfaces with the embedded controller, the main power distribution, the hoisting assembly, and the physical electromagnet.
The interface allocations are the work of J.P. Smith and are detailed in Tables~\ref{tab:interfaces-eeri2} (Level~1 external interfaces) and~\ref{tab:interfaces-eeri2-internal} (Level~2 internal interfaces).
Power enters via I/F~1.2~E from the EEII~I power supply $+13.5\,\text{V}$ DC to the switching circuit and electromagnet coils.
The physical assembly is suspended from the MCTR~II hoisting system via mechanical interface I/F~1.3~M.
Command and feedback signals are exchanged with the REII ESP32-S3 controller across I/F~1.10~S, which carries PWM gate switching command and returns the analog current-sense signal ($V_{\mathrm{ISENSE}} \le 2.048\,\text{V}$).
Internally, the driver stage switches the coils over I/F~1.2.1~E,
the current passes through the ACS712 sensor over I/F~1.2.2~E,
and regulated $+5\,\text{V}$ and ground are shared between the switching and analog conditioning circuit over I/F~1.2.3~E.
Finally, electromagnetic force ($\ge 268.68\,\text{N}$) is applied to the 20\,kg metal plate across mechanical interface I/F~5.0~M.
